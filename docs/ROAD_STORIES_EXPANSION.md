# The Broken Wards — adventure expansion

This expands the playable campaign, not just the length of its written outline.
The base is `main-storyline.md` and the attached *Roads We Leave Behind* story.
Its emotional facts remain: Edmund raised Leigneron; his parents died rescuing
families during a flood; the broken wards connect the regional threats; Walter
paid for the thefts; the Seer knowingly exploited them; Varkhul is the last boss
and survives his retreat. Leigneron is not secretly royal or chosen by prophecy.

## Main story

The existing quests, boss IDs, main-ending dependencies, travel unlocks and
approved illustrated scenes stay in place. New stages are inserted before
retained objective IDs. Existing boss phases, floor warnings and animations are
not replaced by the expansion.

Monster operations have 9–18 total enemies in **three-enemy waves**, rather than
putting a large crowd on screen. Existing 15-enemy scoped hunts remain. The
opening Lena rescue and the finite flagship crew keep their existing sizes.
New work actions use the same post-combat interaction guard as the beacon and
archive; they cannot be armed while unfinished waves remain.

The newly introduced children, relatives and search parties are represented by
environmental evidence and existing speakers' reports, not newly animated NPC
actors. The playable action is clearing/defending their routes and completing
authored site interactions. There is no new escort/pathfinding implementation,
timed civilian death condition, custom puzzle renderer or simulated fire spread.
Do not describe those unimplemented features as present.

## World recovery quests

Eight additional quests are labelled **World quest** in the journal. They appear
on existing NPCs only after the corresponding regional report (or Oakmere's
completed opening). Their exclamation markers and acceptance use the same
milestone check. None is a required detour for the main ending.

| Region | Quest | Continuing problem and learned response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trandum | A Road Is More Than a Sign | Goblins stripped the warning cloths; restore a checked watch rather than promising permanent safety. |
| Narenthil | What Grows After the Silence | Stagnant water remains after the Warden withdraws; open the spillway without destroying living roots. |
| Nardorous | After the Mountain Moves | Rockfall outlasts Stonejaw; use refuge watchers and hold convoys when signals fail. |
| Rindass | Room at the Water | Crowded, frightened herds outlast Krag; separate approaches instead of rebuilding his barriers. |
| Druganwoods | The Water Beneath the Victory | Mine waste still pollutes the spring; obey settling limits instead of blaming the guardian. |
| Portquill | Lights Worth Following | Wreckers can return; teach an answering signal instead of trusting the brightest lamp. |

These use investigations, bounded fights, evidence-based dialogue puzzles and
post-combat work interactions. Their completion scenes and giver conversations
remember the local result. They do **not** implement a new fluid, weather,
terrain-destruction or regional ecology simulation: the repaired-world facts
are represented through quest progress, site interactions and NPC reactions.

## Continuity and climax pass

- The rider is Giles, sent by Cecily after Edmund's letter about the compass.
  He carried the murdered inspector Oswin's warning. These are two people and
  two deaths, not contradictory versions of the same scene.
- New evidence/puzzle stages explain the bell, the Warden's remaining safe path,
  the mountain refuge and the difference between protection and the Seer's
  attempt to force obedience. Leads between regions name the next contact and
  identify the evidence connecting the journey.
- Bruni gives the flood revelation room to breathe. Later evidence explicitly
  separates the parents' deaths from the recent thefts. The Seer offers the
  false reunion the protagonist can now refuse for an established reason.
- Boss introductions now have at least three authored shots. The messenger,
  borrowed-song, river pause, regional transitions and final forge gain staged
  scenes through the existing streaming/fade director. Replaced duplicate scene
  definitions were removed. Approved illustration pages remain in use; new
  dialogue/world shots do not add images to the boot-time decode workload.
- `returning-ember/roads:final-forge-ready` requires the completed
  `the-last-ward/covenant-forge` **stage**, not completion of the entire quest.
  Quest progress, navigation, location text and journal wording support that
  distinction. The whole-quest dependency would be circular because the later
  ward repair requires Varkhul's retreat.
- The dragon remains a free-world encounter, but his main-story approach and
  fresh-defeat objective follow preparation. Previously earned battles and
  completed quests are retained on old saves; they are not forced to replay.

## Pacing contract

The main story has a **9–10 hour target**, with chapter budgets in
`main-storyline.md`. Side stories together target **5–7 hours**. Neither is a
measured claim. This is especially important because familiar routes, skipping
dialogue, local invulnerability and awakened equipment can shorten the game
considerably. Do not enforce the target with forced waits, disabled travel,
boss repetition or inflated HP. If normal first-time play remains too short,
the next iteration needs further distinct encounters/locations, not a changed
number in a document.

## Save and performance integration

- Save version/key remain unchanged. `campaign:roads-unbound-v1` distinguishes
  the expansion and `campaign:roads-covenant-v1` its continuity additions.
  Old completed quests gain only missing new stage keys, without
  awarding their milestone XP or generating choices. Active saves grandfather
  additions behind their last completed legacy stage; future additions remain.
- Active older saves do not grandfather the new preparation dependency. It
  guides them through any missing forge work while retaining earned battle
  stages. Completed saves remain completed, with no repeated rewards.
- Explicit existing partial progress is never overwritten by migration.
  Inventory, housing, equipment, stats, boss state and achievements are untouched.
- Defeated encounter slots and scene markers use existing save flags. The old
  flag-count ceiling gains a finite, content-derived allowance; otherwise a
  sufficiently progressed expanded campaign could reject its own valid save.
- Clues are repeatable and use existing props. Their world placements use
  existing terrain/solid checks. A temporary spatial index avoids scanning all
  world props for every additional clue-placement probe.
- The ordinary chunk manager streams these props. Only the current local
  encounter spawns; it reuses normal enemy art, bounded waves, recovery,
  targeting, allies, cleanup and collision permissions. No new world renderer,
  monster AI, image decoding stage or startup audio was added.
- No new bitmap assets were necessary. Approved style, sprites, atlas geometry,
  portraits and existing lossless WebP paths remain unchanged.

## Verification still required

Compiler checks are separate from gameplay evidence. Focused catalogue and
migration tests are in `tests/roadStories.test.ts`, but must not be executed
without permission. Existing quest-harness tests automatically include the new
quests when that suite is authorised later.

Before a release: authorise the focused quest/save tests, then inspect a normal
new game, a mid-chapter older save, a completed older campaign, each region's
new arena positions, the side quest markers and an Android long-play save with
all finite encounter flags. Check camera return after the added intros and the
normal outside-world enemy population after encounter exit. Measure chapter
times without cheats before treating the pacing targets as achieved.
